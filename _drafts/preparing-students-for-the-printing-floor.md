On 2 October 2026, I facilitated a coordination meeting between UiTM and MatrixMax Systems Sdn Bhd regarding Industrial Training (PMB660) for Bachelor of Printing Technology students.

The discussion covered student placement, suitable production-based tasks, the 24-week training structure, alignment with course learning outcomes, possible pre-placement interviews or assessments, and opportunities for longer-term university–industry collaboration.

## What stayed with me

One question from the discussion stayed with me: **are degree students genuinely prepared and willing to work hands-on in a production environment?**

A degree qualification may indicate academic progress, but it does not automatically mean that a student is ready for the day-to-day realities of a printing floor.

Industrial training places students in an environment where production schedules, machinery, quality requirements, teamwork, discipline, and practical problem-solving matter. The experience can be very different from what students imagine when they think about working in the printing industry.

## A gap in expectations

The discussion also highlighted a possible gap between what students expect from industrial training and what companies actually need from them.

An employer offering a production-based placement may expect a student to participate directly in operational work, learn processes from the ground up, and adapt to the demands of the workplace.

A student, however, may enter the placement expecting work that appears more closely associated with a graduate-level or managerial role.

If these expectations are not discussed beforehand, both the student and the company may begin the placement with different assumptions.

## A possible improvement

One practical idea arising from the discussion is to introduce a simple **pre-placement interview or screening process**.

The purpose would not be to make industrial training unnecessarily difficult. It would help both parties understand each other before the placement begins.

Students could gain a clearer picture of the type of work involved, the working environment, and what the company expects from them. Employers could also better understand the student's interests, readiness, and willingness to participate in production-based activities.

This may help create a better match between student, company, and learning outcomes.

## What I take from this

The meeting reminded me that securing an industrial training placement is only one part of the process.

Preparation before placement may be just as important.

For industrial training to be meaningful, students need more than an available company. They need realistic expectations about the workplace they are entering, and companies need confidence that students understand the nature of the learning experience being offered.

For future placements, I would like to explore how a simple pre-placement conversation or screening process could help create that alignment earlier.
